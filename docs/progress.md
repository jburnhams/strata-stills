## 2026-10-22: raw development, detail measurement, export

- **What the DNG carries** (Mini 4 Pro): ColorMatrix1/2 (illuminants A and D65), AsShotNeutral, BaselineExposure, an Adobe ProfileToneCurve (it maps linear to display-encoded values, gamma included), a NoiseProfile, and an OpcodeList3 with a lens-shading GainMap (centre 1.0, corners up to 5.8x) and a per-channel WarpRectilinear (distortion and lateral colour). LibRaw ignores the opcodes. `develop.py` follows the DNG model with all of them; the raw is about 2 to 3 EV under the camera JPEG, so exposure is automatic (median of the finished picture at 0.42, where the JPEGs sit) with a highlight shoulder.
- **Goal set by the user: beat the camera JPEG in every way.** Compared at 100 % on the Hazelmere shots: cleaner sky and foliage (the JPEG has blotchy noise reduction), more shadow and highlight detail (local tone recovery from the raw), distortion and colour fringing corrected as the maker intended, and much finer texture (roof-tile courses and brick are resolved where the JPEG is mush).
- **A mistake the user caught, and the fix.** The first export used a non-local-means luminance denoise (h up to 6 grey levels, because the shadows are lifted 3 EV) and the roof tiles went waxy. Measured on the roof region: high-frequency energy 0.029 with it against 0.060 to 0.075 without (camera JPEG 0.060). Now 'auto' never smooths luminance in the middle of the frame: only colour noise is blurred (capped at 1.5 px) and a light luminance pass (h <= 3) is blended into the corners where the shading gain is above 2x. Lesson: judge any noise reduction by a texture measure on a textured region and by eye at 100 %, not by how clean the sky looks.
- **Known remaining issues:** visible luminance grain in lifted deep shadows (a coring step on the finest wavelet band, sized by the noise profile, is the next thing to try); slight purple/blue fringing on a few high-contrast edges (lateral colour is corrected by the warp, but not axial fringing: a defringe on edges is the next thing to try); the shadows are cooler than the JPEG's.
- **Ideal export size** (`detail.py`): the sharpness (MTF) of the picture itself is measured from the power spectrum of textured, unclipped tiles of the raw luminance, against a power law fitted to the low frequencies and with the noise floor taken off. It reproduces the known MTF of synthetic Gaussian blurs (sigma 1.0 to 2.5 px) within 20 %. On the Hazelmere DNGs: 50 % contrast is gone at about 0.15 to 0.18 cycles/px (a third of native, 2.8k px across), 30 % at 0.37 (0.75 of native, about 6000 px of 8064), 20 % at 0.43 to 0.44 (0.87). The camera JPEG of the same shot measures worse (30 % limit 0.31 to 0.35 cycles/px on two shots, i.e. 5 to 6k px). Looking at 100 % crops of the native picture and of copies reduced to 0.75, 0.6 and 0.45 and enlarged back, 0.75 is indistinguishable from native, so the recommendation is the 30 % point (`RECOMMEND`), rounded up to a multiple of 64: 6016 px for the first oblique shot (27 MP instead of 48). The DNG is soft rather than empty: contrast falls early but a faint tail of detail goes nearly to Nyquist, which the Wiener sharpening (below) brings back.
- **Sharpening** (`detail.sharpen_gain/sharpen`): a Wiener filter built from the measured MTF (gain m / (m^2 + 0.01), at most 4x), applied to luminance only, with the result clamped to the local range of the original plus 5 % so there are no halos. On a synthetic blurred texture it brings the picture 15 % closer to the truth than the blurred one.
- **Export** (`export.py`, the Export panel on the item page): full-size develop, level at full size (angle measured on the working image), resize (Lanczos above 0.5x, area below), sharpen, write JPEG q95 4:4:4 or 16 bit TIFF with an sRGB profile and the camera's EXIF (orientation reset) to `<project>/exports/`, with a JSON beside it recording each decision. A 48 MP raw takes about 75 s (develop 48 s, lens warp threaded). Exports run in a background thread and the page polls.
- Previews are now developed from the raw by the same recipe at half size (11 s the first time, cached by develop VERSION).

## 2026-10-22: learned denoising on the GPU (after "now too much noise")

- Dropping luminance denoise (to keep texture) left visible grain: the raw is lifted about 3 EV, so the noise at ISO 130 is about 8 to 9 grey levels everywhere in the finished picture (the NoiseProfile says so, and the sky measures the same). The old NLM fixed the grain but was painterly.
- Followed strata-360's finding (docs/progress/2026-10-music-upscale.md there): SCUNet (`scunet_color_real_psnr`, spandrel, 72 MB, from the KAIR release) is the cleanest real-noise denoiser and invents no texture; it must be run before any enlarging and not at full strength everywhere. `denoise_ai.py`: tiled (1024 px, 64 px feathered overlap) fp16 on the RTX 3060 (about 1.8 s a megapixel, under 2 GB of VRAM), tiles whose noise is not visible are skipped, and the result is blended back by a weight map from the noise expected at each pixel (develop.noise_map8: NoiseProfile scaled for the gain, times the sRGB slope; 0 below 1.5 grey levels, 0.85 from 5) so 15 % of the grain stays and clean areas keep exactly what the sensor recorded. The model lightens skies slightly: `keep_tones` gives back the original's broad colour and brightness (1/8-scale difference).
- Crops on the Hazelmere roofs, dark gardens and sky (JPEG | no luma denoise | SCUNet full | SCUNet adaptive | old NLM): SCUNet keeps the tile courses, mortar lines and window frames and removes the grain; NLM is waxy. A pure high-frequency-energy metric cannot rank them (grain counts as texture: 0.059 with grain against 0.013 with SCUNet, 0.041 for the JPEG), so judge by eye at 100 %. Full export of the oblique shot: 111 s (develop 102 s of which the model about 60 s), 6016x4512.
- Remaining: slight halos on the brightest window frames (the luminance sharpening); noise-adaptive weights have not been tried on a cleaner (brighter, low-ISO) shot, where most tiles should be skipped and the export faster.

## 2026-10-22: back to the classical pipeline (colour noise, fringing, highlights, sharpness)

- **User verdict after comparing at 100 %:** Real-ESRGAN "isn't much better" than SCUNet, and the real faults of "ours" against the camera JPEG were purple/magenta fringing and multicolour noise in dark areas (the JPEG's dark tiles are grey, ours were speckled), then lost detail in overexposed areas. The learned models stay available (`develop(denoise='ai')`, `denoise_ai.py`) but are no longer the default; the classical clean-up is. (strata-360 did compare SwinIR, Real-ESRGAN general and x4plus, RealPLKSR, FBCNN and SCUNet; both of the two denoisers tried here smoothed more texture than the classical route, and the Real-ESRGAN supersampled trick barely reduced noise.)
- **Fringing was not the lens correction.** Ablation on the left edge of the frame: pink/blue edge colour is identical with the warp as written, with no warp, with green coefficients on all channels (no lateral colour correction) and with AHD instead of DHT demosaic. The cause is that the lens-shading map lifts the corners up to 5.8x, which amplifies colour noise and edge colour there, while my chroma smoothing was 1.5 px at most. The camera JPEG is neutral grey in those areas.
- **Fix (`develop.clean_colour`)**: luminance and two colour differences; the colour differences go through a guided filter that follows the luminance edges (so colour does not bleed), at 2, 6 or 14 px depending on the noise expected there (the NoiseProfile map); weak colour is cored to grey (threshold a third of the raw noise, since the filter has already averaged most of it away; at 0.9 it muted the real brown of the tiles); colour is rolled off near white (from luminance 0.86) where a clipped channel gives purple and blue fringes. The user pointed out that the scaffolding in the left-edge crop should be blue and the JPEG loses that colour: the blue is kept. `develop.core_luma` removes the finest luminance grain by soft-thresholding the 1 px band at 0.6 x the noise, so strong structure (tile edges, brick lines) passes unchanged, where NLM averaged texture away.
- **Highlights:** measured the raw: the sensor is NOT clipped (0 to 0.11 % of samples per channel on three shots): the camera exposes to protect highlights, so everything the JPEG blows out is in the raw. Flat white walls in the export were my tone mapping. Exposure now protects highlights (the 99.5th percentile may reach linear 1.0; never more than 1 stop under the median rule), and `local_tone` squeezes bright regions as well as shadows (HIGHLIGHTS 0.55). White walls keep their shading and window frames where the JPEG is flat white. Squeezing harder (0.8) or exposing 1.5 stops lower only made them dull grey, so 0.55 stays. Sharpening and highlights are separate: no raw highlight reconstruction is needed on this shoot.
- **Sharpness:** measured over the whole picture the sharpened export is already above the JPEG (MTF50 0.204 against 0.186; 0.32 against 0.29 at 0.3 cycles/px), yet on brick fronts the JPEG looks crisper: its own sharpening adds local contrast that an MTF capped at 1 does not show. `detail.sharpen_gain(amount=)` raises the Wiener gain to a power; chosen by eye at 1.5 ('Crisp') against 1.0 (soft) and 2.0; the Export panel has Restore / Crisp / Extra.
- Time: export of a 48 MP raw 101 s without the model (develop 78 s: the guided filters and local tone are cheap; the lens warp and demosaic dominate).

- **Highlights, round 2 (user chose B, the softer shoulder: WHITE 16, KNEE 0.55, now the default).** Asked for more contrast relative to brightness in the highlights (window frames) without lowering the white level: `develop.highlight_detail` splits the luminance into an edge-preserving base (guided filter) and detail at two scales (2 and 8 px), multiplies the detail by up to 1 + 0.8 where the base is bright (0.62 to 0.92), and puts the base back unchanged. The mean of the bright areas stays 0.865 to 0.870 and nothing new clips; frames, mouldings and the ribs of a corrugated roof stand out more. Compared at 100 % on five bright areas picked by the amount of bright area times structure (out/bright_*.png): 0.8 is the default, 1.5 is 'Strong' in the Export panel, and the camera JPEG blows the same walls flat. Open: luminance grain in light grey walls is visible at 100 % (the coring is tuned for the dark areas).

## 2026-10-22: pipelines, generated variants, automatic JPEG quality

- **User direction:** "it's really important to auto select best options, so hide everything behind an advanced panel"; "instead of export we have generate and it creates another variant of the source with those settings; if we already have one with that setting generate is greyed out, so I can compare versions with the loupe"; "it should be pipeline based with stages; we can then create pipeline configs and auto apply them to source folders to create our preferred variants".
- **Design (`pipeline.py`):** a pipeline is a list of stage settings: develop, level, resize, sharpen, encode (each with its own settings and version; develop first, encode last, others in that order, at most once). Built-in presets `best` (the default: nothing to choose), `archive` (16 bit TIFF at native size) and `web` (2048 px), plus the person's own saved under `<settings home>/pipelines/`. The Advanced panel is flat options laid over a preset (server-side `apply_options`). A generated picture is a variant of its item (`<project>/generated/<item>/<key>.jpg|tif` + JSON), `gen-<key>` in the variant list, can be primary, shows in the loupe; the key is a hash of item, source variant and every stage's settings and version, so the same settings find the existing variant (Generate is greyed out and says Already generated; Show it selects it). Generating always starts from a source variant, never from a generated one. Rules in project.json (`auto`: source id or `*` plus preset) are edited with the Auto-generate checkboxes on a source's page; Generate N missing runs them in a thread with progress, stop and a per-picture failure list, and is safe to press again after adding photos.
- **Automatic JPEG quality (`jpeg_quality.py`):** measured on the 27 MP export: the picture's own noise in smooth areas is 1.85 grey levels and JPEG at q95 adds 1.74 of RMS error (as much as the noise), q98 adds 0.79, q99 0.47, q100 0.30 (36 MB against 24 MB at q98 and 17.8 MB at q95). SSIM is no use as a target (0.991 at q95 on any textured picture). The rule: encode the 16 most textured and 8 smoothest 512 px tiles (16-aligned, so encoded exactly as inside the whole picture) at trial qualities and binary-search the lowest quality in 88..99 whose luminance RMS error is at most 0.45 x the noise sigma of the smooth tiles (Haar MAD estimate), but not below 0.45 or above 1.5 grey levels. A very clean smooth picture needs only about q90, a slightly noisy one q99, a grainy one q97. The record of each generated picture says the quality chosen, the error, the target and the noise.
- **Pillow bug found by the tests:** `save(optimize=True)` fails with 'Suspension not allowed here' when the encoder buffer is smaller than the file (an 800x600 noise picture was enough); `ImageFile.MAXBLOCK` is raised in both encode paths. Real exports of noisy pictures would have hit it.
- **JPEG encoders compared** (18 real 512 px tiles, equal luminance error): Pillow's libjpeg-turbo (in use) and imagecodecs' jpeg8 are identical; a lossless mozjpeg re-pack of the same JPEG is 5 to 7 % smaller with bit-identical pixels but 1 to 2 s a tile (minutes for a 27 MP picture); jpegli (pyjpegli) is 8 to 14 % smaller at equal luminance error but has higher chroma error (about 1.0 against 0.7), so it is not shown to be better without a perceptual metric (butteraugli is not installed). The headroom inside JPEG is modest; larger savings need another format (JPEG XL, AVIF) at a cost in compatibility. The quality rule is encoder-agnostic (`_encode`), so a 'smaller file' option can be added.
- **Not done:** the loupe still takes its crops from a 4096 px working picture even for a generated variant (a 6016 px export is reduced to compare at one scale); full-resolution crops of generated and source JPEG variants would show the extra detail.

The highlight-contrast boost was a minor cause (5 %); a noise-aware version of it cancelled the boost entirely because its threshold came from the pixel noise, not the much lower noise within a band. Result: bright walls 2.59 | 4.03 (JPEG 2.53 | 4.01), dark 0.57 | 0.61 (JPEG 0.79 | 0.71), mid 0.94 | 0.91 (JPEG 1.28 | 1.07), at a cost of 3 to 6 % of edge strength (p95 gradient in window frames, mouldings, corrugated ribs and roof tiles). ## 2026-10-22: roof tiles check, and the export sharpening was adding grain

- **Roof check (user request):** the first automatic picker chose a lawn, paving and flat felt roofs, so I made gridded overviews of the shots and chose real tiled and slate roofs by eye. With the export sharpening included, tile detail (std of the 1-3 px luminance band) was +9 to +42 % over the camera JPEG on every roof with real texture and 7 to 16 % below the version before the band coring (the cost of the coring; at native scale the orange clay tiles, the worst case, kept all their courses and joints, only a calmer surface).
- **The user asked whether the sharpening adds noise: it did, a lot, and I had never measured it** (all my grain numbers were of the developed picture, before sharpening). On the export path (resize to 6016, sharpen amount 1.5), the finest-scale (0.5 to 1 px) grain in flat patches rose from 1.24 / 1.91 / 3.08 (dark / mid / bright) to 3.65 / 5.69 / 6.35, against 1.62 / 2.49 / 2.99 for the camera JPEG. Tile detail rose 33 % at the same time (8.0 to 10.5, JPEG 10.0). Cause: the Wiener gain m/(m^2 + 0.01) climbs to its cap at the highest frequencies, where the measured MTF is lowest and there is mostly noise.
- **Fix (`detail.sharpen_gain`):** a larger noise term (0.04) and a roll-off of the boost between 0.12 and 0.30 cycles/pixel of the output, and the gain is scaled by (1 + noise) so it is exactly 1 where nothing is blurred (a larger noise term otherwise took 4 % off the broad tones). With a noise term the filter also attenuates the frequencies that are mostly noise (gain below 1 there), which the roll-off then eases back to 1. Scanned noise terms 0.01 to 0.10 and roll-offs from 0.10 to 0.40: roll-off starting at 0.10 to 0.12 holds tile detail (10.3 to 10.7) while a noise term alone (0.10) cost tile detail (9.2) for less grain reduction. Result: finest-scale grain 1.48 / 2.30 / 4.06 (dark / mid / bright; JPEG 1.62 / 2.49 / 2.99), tile detail 10.7 (JPEG 10.0), p95 edge gradient on window frames and mouldings 1.51 (JPEG 1.33, old filter 1.70). Bright walls are still about a third above the JPEG's grain: a brightness-dependent roll-off is the next thing to try. Pipeline sharpen stage version 2, so existing generated pictures are not mistaken for the new recipe.

- **Brightness-aware sharpening (user: 'lets try on bright walls').** `detail.sharpen_picture` blends a tighter roll-off (0.06 to 0.16 cycles/px) into the sharpening where the smoothed luminance is bright (smoothstep from 0.60 over 0.28): bright-wall grain 4.06 -> 3.58 against the JPEG's 2.99, bright-wall structure detail 16.9 (JPEG 15.4), p95 edge gradient on window frames and mouldings 1.43 (JPEG 1.29), roof tile detail unchanged (10.7). Scanned 0.10-0.24, 0.08-0.20, 0.06-0.16: tighter is better down to there with diminishing returns. The sharpen stage is version 3. The earlier crop sets (5 bright-wall areas, 8 roofs) were redone at the same native-scale positions: the 1-3 px detail with the final sharpening is within 0 to 6 % of the old filter on the roofs and 2 to 11 % lower on bright walls (partly grain: the corrugated roof's ribs are as crisp but no longer speckled), and above the camera JPEG everywhere (roofs +9 to +42 %).

## 2026-10-22: several pictures at once

- **Design:** `pipeline.apply_rules(workers=)` makes pictures in a thread pool (one picture per thread, distinct files, nothing shared). The CPU parts (LibRaw decode, levelling, JPEG encode, file and network reads; all release the GIL) overlap the card's work; only the GPU parts hold a device slot (`gpu.slot()`: a semaphore sized from the card's memory in steps of 6.5 GB, so one on a 12 GB card, three on a 24 GB one; 1 on MPS; STRATASTILLS_GPU_SLOTS overrides), so memory never overruns. The raw is decoded BEFORE the slot is taken. Default workers (`resources.auto_workers`): 2 with a GPU; without one, one picture per 4 cores and as many as 80 % of the memory holds at about 7 GB each (at most 6). The apply endpoint takes `workers=` and reports how many ran.
- **Bug the tests found:** the slot semaphore was created lazily without a lock, so two threads at the start of a batch could create two semaphores and a release went to the wrong one ('Semaphore released too many times', one picture failed). Now created under a lock and released on the object that was acquired.
- **Measured** (scripts/bench_parallel.py: 8 different 48 MP shots, a warm-up first; the machine was busy with other work, so treat as noisy): 1 at once 99.8 s, 2 at once 68.5 s (x1.46), 3 at once 81.7 s (x1.22), 4 at once 71.3 and 67.8 s (x1.4 to 1.5); a picture takes 12.5 s alone, 17 s with two in flight, 28 to 35 s with three or four (they queue for the one slot). Peak VRAM 5.5 GB throughout. So 2 at once is the default: a 1.45x throughput gain from overlapping CPU work with the card; more than that only adds latency until a bigger card allows more slots. (The first runs of the first attempt came back in 0.3 s because an earlier aborted launch had already made those pictures: the key finds them.)
- **Not measured:** the CPU-only path in parallel, and MPS.

## Colour: the "very orange" sunlit roof (develop VERSION 9)

Cause, measured against the camera JPEG on 0037 (Lab, orange area = JPEG hue 40-85, chroma > 20): our orange was chroma 39 vs 30 and lightness 62 vs 71. The per-channel profile tone curve darkens the weaker channels most, so strong colours get over-saturated and darker. The DNG's own ProfileHueSatMap (36x10, hue shift -17..+6 deg, saturation scale 0.83..1.20) is now applied in ProPhoto between the colour matrix and the exposure/tone steps (CPU reference + GPU, tests agree); it makes hues faithful but not less saturated (chroma 41). Adobe's RGBTone (curve on max and min channel, middle in proportion) keeps hue but lifted chroma/lightness nothing useful either (a_ratio 0.75 vs 0.45 JPEG: redder), so per-channel stays the default (TONE_BLEND = 1). What fixed it: `limit_chroma` (knee 0.20, ceiling 0.45 in display RGB distance from same-luma grey, luma kept): top-decile chroma vs JPEG on 0037/0092/0101/0104 went 39/35, 33/31, 28/26, 51/43 -> 36.6/35.3, 29.4/30.9, 26.9/25.6, 41.2/42.6. Soft colours are untouched. Remaining: sunlit orange is ~8 lightness units darker than the JPEG (crops in docs/orange_0037.png). Scripts: scripts/colour_eval.py, chroma_sweep.py, orange_crops.py.
Search: no public DJI Mini 4 Pro colour profile / tone curve exists; Adobe ships camera-matching profiles (Adobe Standard etc.), darktable lacks opcode 9 (lens shading), and the DNG's embedded profile is the right source.

## Levelling reliability
`estimate_level` now also estimates the tilt in each quadrant; if those (with >= 8 lines each, at least 3 of them) differ by more than 1.5 degrees (`MAX_SPREAD_DEG`) it refuses, saying the parts disagree. Pipelines no longer level straight-down shots unless the level stage has `nadir: true` (default false; their lines swung 1-2 degrees from crop to crop); the standalone Levelled transform still does. Level version 2.

## One 'auto' size per folder (sizeplan.py)
'auto' no longer uses each picture's own measured size: it is the median detail limit over a sample (<= 10, evenly spread) of the source folder's pictures of the same native size, rounded up to a multiple of 64 on the long side, and the output dimensions are multiples of 16 (else 8, 4, 2) where the aspect ratio allows (8064x6048 -> 6016x4512 style). Cached in the project cache keyed by sample + develop version. Sizes asked for in pixels are exact. Never below 50% of the native size (MIN_SHARE). Resize stage version bumped.

## Linear DNGs and input profiles (lineardng.py, inputs.py)
The DJI "-Pano" DNGs are Lightroom Classic panorama merges: LINEAR_RAW (demosaiced RGB, JPEG XL tiles), which LibRaw refuses. `lineardng.read` gives develop the same thing LibRaw would: per-channel black/white (BlackLevel is RATIONAL here), the OpcodeList2 MapPolynomial per plane (Lightroom stores merges in a non-linear encoding; this makes them linear), then white balance 1/AsShotNeutral (checked: neutral pixels of the embedded preview come out at the AsShotNeutral ratio after the polynomial). The merge's empty curved borders (MASK page) are cropped to the biggest inscribed rectangle. Pictures bigger than the card (gpu.max_pixels(): 90 % of VRAM over the measured 133 bytes per pixel; 116 MP thrashes through system memory for minutes) are area-averaged down to fit, so the pano develops at ~83 % of native, in about 3-4 minutes.
Lightroom's merge carries the Adobe Standard profile instead of the camera's own (other ColorMatrix, a 90x30 hue/sat map with saturation scale up to 2 and value scale up to 1.9, a ProfileLookTable, no ProfileToneCurve: Lightroom's renderer adds its default curve). Using those tables made the panoramas heavily over-saturated and orange, so `inputs.adjust_tags` puts the camera's own profile back (colour matrices, hue/sat map, tone curve: `dji_fc8482_profile.json`, extracted from its raw DNGs) when ProfileName is Adobe Standard; the white balance, noise profile and levels stay the file's. The Adobe look table is ignored. `inputs.describe(path)` classifies a file (raw / linear-dng / rendered, make, model, profile) and is recorded as `input` in each generated picture's sidecar. To add a device: an entry in `inputs.PROFILES` (and a reader if its files are a new kind) plus a test.
After the swap: median HSV saturation pano 55 vs the regular raw of the same scene 46 vs Lightroom's preview 29 (docs/pano_0046_compare.jpg: ours above, Lightroom's preview below).

## Matching a panorama's levels to its frames (match.py)
A Lightroom merge is exposed by its own content, so it sat 3-7 lightness units off the frames it was made from (and ~1.4 a* greener on neutrals). The develop stage's `match` setting (default 'auto', only for linear DNGs; not part of the key unless 'off') develops the shot the panorama is named after (then others by time stamp, window -150..+15 s, until 80 points agree) at half size, finds the place with SIFT + RANSAC homography, samples a grid of FLAT patches near agreeing features (patches over edges differ by any misalignment; keypoint patches are mostly edges: that biased the first attempt), and fits a monotone curve per channel by quantile matching (binned medians of y|x flatten with the scatter between views: regression to the mean). Result against our generated frames (flat patches): 0046 pano dL +0.2, da 0.0, db +0.2, chroma ratio 1.05; 0077 dL +0.06, chroma 1.01 (before: +2.8/-4.6, da -1.0/-1.2, db -1.5/-2.6). Check with scripts/pano_match_check.py. Note the frames themselves differ from each other by a few L units (each is exposed by its own content).

## Tiled GPU development (gpu_tiled.py) and panorama size
A 116 MP linear DNG needs ~15 GB on the card (12 GB); it did not fail, it thrashed through system memory for minutes, and the earlier fix averaged it down to fit (losing detail a merge of many frames can carry). `gpu_tiled.develop_tiled` does the same recipe in 2048 px tiles with a 128 px margin: pass A per tile (shading gain, the lens warp of just the source part the tile samples, colour) into a float32 array in system memory; global numbers (local-tone base on a 1200 px copy and its median, exposure, noise estimate) from a reduced copy / every 8th pixel; pass B per tile with the margin through `develop.gpu_tail` (the shared tail of both paths; widest reach ~90 px). Used when the picture is over `gpu.max_pixels()`; an opcode order it cannot do raises Unsupported and a reduced copy is developed instead. Against the whole-picture version on a 48 MP raw: mean difference 0.17 of 255 levels, 0.1 % of pixels differ by more than 5 levels, no seams, low-frequency content within 0.6 levels, and no slower (6.9 s vs 7.4 s). The panoramas now develop at native size (13072x6305, 12874x7311, 9389x5824) in about a minute each.
Merged panoramas (linear DNG) now have a 75 % floor on the 'auto' size (others 50 %): their MTF30 comes out at 43-55 % of their native size (Lightroom's merge resamples), so the measurement alone would give outputs smaller than one frame. A panorama's default primary variant is now its raw (linear DNG), not its JPEG.

## Tiling for everything? (measured, RTX 3060 12 GB, 8 different 48 MP raws, scripts/bench_parallel.py with STRATASTILLS_MAX_PIXELS=1000 to force tiles and STRATASTILLS_GPU_SLOTS=3)
whole picture (1 slot): 1 at once 78 s (9.8 s each), 2: 58 s, 3: 58 s (7.2 s each), peak VRAM 6.7 GB. Tiled (3 slots): 1 at once 99 s (12.3 s each), 2: 65 s, 3: 57 s, 4: 51 s (6.4 s each), peak VRAM 2.1 GB. So tiling is ~11 % faster at its best (4 at once) than the best whole-picture batch, 25 % slower for a lone picture, and uses a third of the card memory; the rest of the time per picture is CPU (decode, encode, files), which more GPU residency does not help. Kept: whole picture when it fits, tiles when it does not (or by STRATASTILLS_MAX_PIXELS); slots are still sized for whole pictures (set STRATASTILLS_GPU_SLOTS for tiled batches).

Automatic: `gpu.batch(workers)` (used by `apply_rules`) puts a batch in tile mode when the card cannot hold that many whole pictures (`tiling_pays`); slots are then sized from `TILE_PEAK_BYTES` (2.7 GB: 4 on the 12 GB card, 8 on 24 GB, 2 on 6 GB) instead of `PEAK_BYTES`, `auto_workers` with a GPU is `gpu.plan_workers()` (tile slots + 1, at most 4, limited by system memory at 2.5 GB a picture), and a lone picture still goes through whole. The decode of the next raw already overlaps the card's work: it runs outside the slot, and with more workers than slots the extra ones are decoding/encoding meanwhile. Real batch on the 12 GB card: 16 pictures including two panoramas, 4 workers, 124 s. MPS (tile_slots 2, workers 3) and CPU-only batches are untested.

## Tile size follows the memory (gpu.tile_size, resources.cpu_tile) and a tiled NumPy path (cpu_tiled.py)
Card memory of a tiled picture scales with the tile area: 0.91 GB at 2048 px tiles (+128 margin), 0.29 at 1024, 0.11 at 512, 0.06 at 256 (about 175 B per pixel of tile plus margin); time on the 12 GB card for a 48 MP raw: 7.2 s, 8.0 s, 13 s, 37 s. `gpu.tile_size()` takes the largest tile (multiples of 64, 256 to 2048) that fits 90 % of the card over the slots in use, so a small card gets small tiles (`STRATASTILLS_TILE` overrides).
CPU only: the whole-picture NumPy development peaks at 145 B/px (8.3 GB for 48 MP; a 116 MP panorama would need 17 GB), so when a picture does not fit its share of system memory (`resources.budget()`: 70 % of RAM over the pictures being made at once) `cpu_tiled.develop_tiled` develops it in tiles of the size the budget allows (`resources.cpu_tile`). Measured with scripts/tile_memory.py (peak working set, 48 MP): whole 8.26 GB, tiles of 2048 1.96 GB (147 B/px of tile and margin, 16 B/px above the decoded raw), tiles of 1024 and 512 1.17 GB (24 B/px: the picture itself); the time cost on this machine (CPU contended by other work while measuring): about +20 to +30 % at 2048, +80 % at 1024, much more at 512. Tests hold it to the whole-picture result (test_gpu.py, integration/test_pipeline_end_to_end.py).

## Other cameras: Sony A7C (ARW) (inputs.synthesize_tags, scripts/build_profile_bundle.py, scripts/fit_tone_curve.py)
Test set: \SERVER\Raw\2023\2023-01-29, 170 ARW+JPG pairs, Sony ILCE-7C, Samyang AF 50mm F1.4 II, 6024x4024 decoded / 6000x4000 picture. `scan` and LibRaw already handled the files; the development was wrong: an ARW has no DNG colour tags, so develop fell back to "camera RGB is sRGB" with no tone curve (median saturation 23 against the camera JPEG's 56, flat).
Looked at what others ship (shallow clones, 2026-10-23): RawTherapee has `rtdata/dcpprofiles/SONY ILCE-7C.dcp` (matrices for illuminants A and D65 plus Adobe's hue/saturation maps, public domain) and darktable has per-ISO noise fits for the A7C (`data/noiseprofiles.json`) and its own filmic/sigmoid style for it; neither has a lens profile for this lens (lensfun lists the body, not the AF 50 f/1.4 II). LightCraft (Rust, MIT/Apache) derives a camera profile by pairing each raw with its own camera JPEG, quantile-matching the luminance (the tone curve) and fitting a chromaticity matrix; it also decodes Sony's embedded lens tables.
What was done:
- `inputs.synthesize_tags` (called from `develop.read_dng_tags`): a raw that is not a DNG gets the as-shot white balance from LibRaw and the colour from its device profile bundle (`PROFILES` entry `('sony', 'ilce-7c')`), else LibRaw's XYZ->camera matrix. The noise profile comes from the bundle's NoiseTable at the shot's ISO (interpolated log-log).
- `scripts/build_profile_bundle.py` builds a bundle from a DCP (+ darktable's noise table). The DCP's hue/saturation maps are left out by default: with them median chroma was 1.9x the camera JPEG's and greens went olive, matrices alone 1.15x (same finding as the Adobe Standard maps on the DJI panoramas).
- `scripts/fit_tone_curve.py` fits `ProfileToneCurve` from 24 of the 170 pairs (linear luminance of our development at ev 0 against the JPEG's, 48 equal-count slices, clipped pixels left out). After it the colour and contrast of DSC00282 are close to the camera JPEG; our picture is brighter only because `auto_ev` targets a fixed median.
- `develop._raw_u16` cuts non-DNG raws to the maker's active area (ARW 6024x4024 -> 6000x4000, as the camera JPEG), since there is no DefaultCrop tag.
Open: the greens of this indoor shot are still a little olive against the JPEG (white balance tint); a fitted chromaticity matrix (LightCraft's approach) would close it. The ARW carries Sony's own shading, lateral CA and distortion tables (raw SubIFD tags 0x7031/2, 0x7034/5, 0x7036/7; the distortion table here has setting 0): applying them is the lens-correction path, not done. Not checked on the GPU path (no torch in this venv).

## What the ARW's metadata says about the light (A7C, 30 pairs)
The EXIF has BrightnessValue (APEX scene brightness: -3.1 in the dimmest frame, 8.3 in sun), ISO, shutter, aperture, ExposureBias (-0.3 throughout), metering mode (5, multi-segment) and the maker note has the DRO setting (tag 0xb025, = 3 on all of them), creative style (0xb020), HDR/high-ISO NR settings (0x2009/0x200a), and Sony's encrypted blocks 0x9400-0x940f (more of the same; not decoded here). The residual of exposure=camera against the JPEG (ours minus the JPEG's lightness) correlates -0.78 with BrightnessValue (+0.56 with log ISO): ours is too bright in dim scenes and too dark in sunlit ones, as a scene-adaptive tone (DRO) would do. Linear fit dL = a + b*Bv leaves less than the raw spread; DRO acts on the picture's own histogram, so metadata only predicts part of it.

## exposure: match (tonematch.py): the tone of the camera's own JPEG, picture by picture, RawTherapee's approach
`develop` stage setting `exposure: 'match'` (Advanced panel: "Match the camera's JPEG"). The raw is developed at half size with no tone curve, the JPEG inside the raw (`rawpy.extract_thumb`, so any raw LibRaw reads; skipped when it is under 19200 px or does not agree with the raw: rank correlation of the luminances < 0.9) is turned the way that agrees best, and the curve that takes our luminance onto the preview's is found by quantile matching (48 equal-count slices, the method of RawTherapee's "auto-matched tone curve", written from its description). Then the colour: a flat curve per channel makes a strong shadow lift far too colourful (chroma ratio 1.9 against the JPEG), so a chroma scale over display luminance (12 slices: preview's colour over ours after the curve) is measured too and applied before `limit_chroma` (`develop.chroma_scale`, `gpu.chroma_scale`; CPU, CPU-tiled, GPU, GPU-tiled). Both are kept in the record of the picture (`tone`: source, curve, chroma), as plain numbers, for tweaking later; `develop(tone=)` takes a list (a curve) or that dict. This aims at DRO parity (the JPEG's look including Sony's Dynamic Range Optimizer); the DRO-off look LightCraft and Lightroom/darktable use is the fixed `camera` exposure.
Parity (scripts/parity_jpeg.py, A7C, the same 30 of 170, half size, CIELAB at 1/8, medians): `camera` fixed curve: dL -0.6, worst |dL| 11.4, dE 5.15, chroma ratio 1.21; `match`: dL +0.4, worst |dL| 0.4, dE 2.2, chroma ratio 0.96, hue -3.7 degrees (a remaining bias: white balance or the colour matrix). CPU and GPU paths run (the GPU one on the RTX 3060); the Mac MPS path was not.
Found on the way: non-DNG raws were not turned upright (LibRaw `user_flip=0`; portrait ARWs came out on their side, which showed as dE 20-30 outliers). `_raw_u16` now applies the file's orientation (read before `postprocess`: with user_flip=0 LibRaw overwrites it) for non-DNG raws. The DJI DNGs are always upright and unchanged.

## Local tone and the colour roll-off were fighting the matched tone; how much per-device data is needed
The DJI-era steps (local tone `recover` 0.35, the colour roll-off `limit_chroma`, highlight contrast, the shoulder, the auto-exposure target) were tuned on drone DNGs. With `exposure: match` the tone is the camera's own global curve and colour scale, and the local tone step on top of it moved highlights and shadows away from the camera's JPEG, and the roll-off undid the matched colour. So the develop stage has a `recover` setting ('auto' = 0.35, but 0 with `match`) and the roll-off is skipped when a matched look is present. Candidates (25, medians) before -> after: colour difference 2.11 -> 1.54, 90th percentile 5.70 -> 3.92, saturated colours 7.38 -> 3.16, highlights 4.17 -> 3.09; DSC00449-00451 4.1/3.5/3.7 -> 2.2-2.4; DSC00321 saturated 15.8 -> 7.4. The shoulder made no difference. The look table was refitted on the new pipeline (136 pictures, 34 held out): held out dE 1.62 -> 1.54, 90th percentile 4.19 -> 3.88, saturated 6.49 -> 4.21 (31 better, 0 worse). Against the other programs on the candidates there is no picture left where darktable or RawTherapee is better on colour difference, 90th percentile, saturated colours or shadows; they are better only in the highlights of DSC00444/445 (thin bright gold edges) and 449-451 (lights).
White balance: not the issue. Near-neutral pixels match the camera JPEG to within 1 % on every one of the 170 pictures, with no trend against the as-shot colour temperature. The warm indoor frames (17 of 170 with a warm light) differ in the HUE of orange and yellow-green (+10-15 and -12 degrees before the look table); a separate look table for them, learnt on the 17 and held out 4 ways, gained only about 5 % there, so it was not built.
Vignetting: the ratio of our luminance to the camera JPEG's by distance from the centre is within +-0.03 stop at every radius (pooled median over the 170), per picture centre-to-corner spread -0.05..+0.06: the JPEG has the same vignetting as the raw. The ARW carries Sony's shading table (0x7031/0x7032) for a correction that the camera does not apply to these JPEGs; applying it is a lens-correction step beyond the camera's rendering.
How much is per device (25 candidates, `match` throughout, medians): DCP matrices + look table + noise table 1.54 / 3.92 / 3.16 (colour difference / 90th / saturated); DCP matrices only 1.62 / 4.12 / 4.20; no device profile at all (LibRaw's own matrix) 2.00 / 3.94 / 4.20, hue +1.4 degrees. ## Lens correction from the file's own data (lenscorr.py; develop `lens: auto|off`)
Both RawTherapee (rtengine/lensmetadata.cc, adapted from darktable) and darktable (iop/lens.cc + common/exif.cc; plus lensfun) apply the camera's own correction tables when the raw carries them, so ours does too. The A7C ARW carries, in its raw IFD, shading (0x7032), lateral colour (0x7035: red, then blue) and distortion (0x7037), 16 knots at radius (i + 0.5) / 15 of the half diagonal; conventions of those programs (distortion 1 + d 2^-14, colour 1 + c 2^-21, shading gain 1 / 2^(0.5 - 2^(v 2^-13 - 1))). They are made into the DNG opcodes the development already applies (GainMap on a 65x65 grid, WarpRectilinear with a degree-3 polynomial of r^2 per plane, fit error under 3e-4), so CPU, CPU-tiled, GPU and GPU-tiled all do it with no new path (`inputs.synthesize_tags`; for a DNG the file's own opcodes are used as before). For these frames the shading gain reaches 1.70 (0.77 stop) in the corner and distortion is under 1 %. Check: the median over 40 pictures of log2 luminance against the centre by radius falls from +0.2 in the middle to -0.49 at the corner without it and stays at +0.28..+0.30 from the middle to the corner with it. The A7C JPEGs are NOT corrected for shading (our uncorrected development matches them to 0.03 stop at every radius), so the parity numbers are measured with `lens: off` (`parity_jpeg.py --lens off`, the default there; DJI's JPEGs are corrected, so DJI is measured with `--lens auto`); the pipeline default is `auto`, so the generated picture has flat corners and the camera JPEG does not.
`exposure: auto` (the default) now means the tone of the camera's own JPEG when the raw has a usable preview (every raw LibRaw reads has one; the DJI DNGs carry a 960x720 one), with the fixed lightness rule as the fallback (linear DNG panorama merges, files without a preview). ## The matched tone on the card (develop(tone='match'), no second development)
The pipeline used to find the matched tone with `tonematch.curve_for`: a second, half-size development of the picture on the CPU (NumPy) and a second raw decode, only to get a few dozen numbers (2.6 s of the 10.3 s of one A7C generate; profile: run_develop 6.85 s, of which curve_for 2.64 s). Now `develop(..., tone='match')` (ev 'auto' or 'match', recover 'auto') does it itself: on the card, right after the colour step, the linear picture is shrunk there (`gpu.resize_area`) to the 384 px the match works at, fitted on the CPU against the JPEG inside the raw (`develop._match_on_device`, a few ms), and the development goes on with that curve; the curve comes back in `analysis['tone']` for the picture's record. Routes without a card on hand (CPU only, AI denoise, tiles for a picture too big for the card) get the curve the old way (`_match_on_cpu`). No usable preview: `analysis['tone']` is None and `auto` goes on with the fixed lightness rule (and recover 0.35), `match` with the camera exposure. Result: one generate of an A7C picture 10.3 s -> 4.2 s (run_develop 6.85 -> 3.49 s); the 25 A7C candidates unchanged through the pipeline's own route (0 flagged, median colour difference 1.54, 90th percentile 3.92 -> 3.85). `parity_jpeg.py` now uses the same route.

## Denoise: one approach, a calibration per device (`NoiseScale`, scripts/fit_noise_scale.py)

## Sharpening: overshoot and the DJI gap (scripts/sharpen_sweep.py, docs/sharpening_comparison.md)
Method: `sharpen_sweep.py cache` runs the pipeline up to the sharpen stage once per candidate (CPU, 3000 px, 16 bit, with the measured MTF); `apply` then tries any number of sharpening variants on those in seconds (`--amount`, `--patch detail.NAME=value`; `detail.sharpen_gain` looks its defaults up at call time so that patches reach them); `compare_detail.py --report` scores them against RawTherapee (Auto-Matched), darktable and the camera JPEG with per-ISO tables and per-picture win/tie/loss counts. New metric `ring` (distance from a 7x7 median in the 4 px around the strongest edges: the narrow lobes of ringing).
Findings (A7C 46 pictures, DJI 13, medians): (1) the overshoot metric `halo` of the UNSHARPENED A7C pictures is already 17.7 (RawTherapee 18.5, the old default 19.5): the old clamp (0.05 of the range beyond the local 5x5 range) was letting through nearly all of the excess; with 0 it is 17.0 at amount 2 and 16.3 at amount 3 (both written at q98; the final numbers below are at the product's q90). (2) The sharpening boost was rolled off too early (0.12-0.30 cycles/px): widening it to 0.2-0.5 (bright areas 0.06-0.16 -> 0.1-0.3) is where the DJI sharpness comes from. (3) Flat-area noise and colour noise do not move with any of this (the activity mask keeps the boost out of flat areas): every variant has the same noise column. (4) `ring` rises with sharpness in every variant (A7C 4.62 -> 4.64 at the new default, RawTherapee 4.56; DJI 13.25 -> 14.14, RawTherapee 13.44): nothing found that sharpens like RawTherapee with less ringing; at 100 % the new default shows no halos on blossom, edges or light trails.
New default (amount 3.0, `ROLLOFF` (0.2, 0.5), `ROLLOFF_BRIGHT` (0.1, 0.3), `OVERSHOOT` 0; sharpen stage VERSION 5; the amount range is now 0-4 and the Advanced panel offers Restore 1, Crisp 2, Best 3, Extra 3.5). Written by the product's encoder (JPEG q90), medians: A7C sharp 17.2 -> 18.8 (RawTherapee 15.9, darktable 17.4, camera 18.5), halo 19.7 -> 17.6 (18.5), detail 3.51 -> 3.81 (2.47); DJI sharp 47.2 -> 52.0 (RawTherapee 51.6; the camera JPEG, 65.7, is still far ahead), halo 44.0 -> 34.5 (42.1), detail 7.2 -> 8.3 (2.1). Behind RawTherapee still: `ring` (A7C +5 %, DJI +7 %) and, in the 251-800 ISO band of the A7C set, `halo` (17.7 against 16.6, with 28 % more sharpness). Full tables: docs/sharpening_comparison.md.
**The noise difference between machines was the JPEG quality.** The first sweeps here wrote q98 JPEGs and measured flat-area noise 0.97 (A7C median) against the 0.58 of the Windows renders; the cause is not the CPU or GPU route (the old default re-made here at q90 gives 0.62 on the A7C set and 1.16 on the DJI set, the Windows renders' 0.58 and 1.16, with sharpness 17.2/47.2 against 17.4/47.1) but the JPEG quantisation: the built-in presets write q90, and q90 alone takes the flat-area noise of one picture from 0.76 to 0.42 (a picture's own file size falls from 2.4 MB to 0.5 MB). Rule for the comparison scripts: noise (and detail, which divides by it) is only comparable between files written at the same quality; `sharpen_sweep.py apply` now uses the product's encoder and quality.
Not verified: judged on the cached 3000 px pictures, not full-size exports; the GPU route of the GTX 1050 here is broken (see the log in the docs repo), so CPU/GPU parity was not tested on this machine, only that the CPU route reproduces the Windows (GPU) renders.
**Do the others use q90? No: they write higher quality, so q90 flatters our noise.** Estimated from the luminance quantisation tables (libjpeg scaling): the built-in presets q90 (4:4:4); RawTherapee about q92 (4:4:4); darktable about q95 (4:4:4); the camera JPEGs about q96 (A7C, 4:2:2) and q99 (DJI, 4:2:0). Re-written at q95 (the new default, `sharpen_sweep.py apply --quality 95`), medians against RawTherapee / darktable / camera: A7C noise 0.73 (0.96 / 2.62 / 0.97), detail 3.19 (2.47 / 1.81 / 3.33, so now just under the camera's), halo 16.9 (18.5 / 19.3 / 20.1), ring 4.72 (+3.5 % on RawTherapee), sharp 18.9; DJI noise 1.27 (4.4 / 4.0 / 1.9), detail 8.1 (2.1 / 1.9 / 7.5), halo 32.8, ring 14.23 (+6 %), sharp 52.0. Ahead of RawTherapee and darktable on noise at the same quality, so the conclusions stand, but the q90 columns of the tables above overstate the lead in noise and detail by about 0.1 and 0.4 (A7C).

## Image circle: an APS-C lens on the full frame body is cropped to what it covers (`develop frame: auto`)
The E 50mm F1.8 OSS (crop factor 1.534 in lensfun) is an APS-C lens and the A7C is full frame: the lens lights a circle, and outside it the picture is black falloff (about 5 stops in the corners, no correction can bring it back; lensfun only recovers about 1). The camera's own JPEG is the full 6000x4000 with the dark corners. Rule (`lensfundb.image_circle`, no setting needed): when the lens's crop factor is more than 8 % above the sensor's (from `FocalLengthIn35mmFilm / FocalLength`), the picture is cropped, centred and in the same shape, to the largest rectangle inside the circle: sensor crop / lens crop = 0.652 (3911x2608 from 6000x4000, the camera's own APS-C mode). Lenses for the sensor (all the others in the candidate set), lenses lensfun does not know and files without the 35 mm equivalent keep the whole picture. `frame: full` turns it off for debugging; the crop is recorded as `frame` in the generated picture's record. DSC00072 (E 50, ISO 100 band): the black circle edge is gone from the whole frame.
Also: lensfun's shading and distortion for such a lens are now evaluated with the real sensor crop factor (`corrections_from_exif` passed none, so the lens was treated as made for the sensor it sat on).

**Roll-off shape does not move ringing.** Tried a smoothstep instead of the linear fade of the boost over `ROLLOFF` (sweep on the cached A7C and DJI pictures, q95), medians: DJI sharp 52.03 -> 52.17, ring 14.23 -> 14.27; A7C sharp 18.87 -> 18.90, ring 4.72 -> 4.75; and with amount 3.5 on top, DJI 53.4 / 14.47 and A7C 19.5 / 4.70. ## GPU route on a small shared card (GTX 1050, 2 GB)
The planner used the card's total memory; with the desktop on it only ~1.6 GB is free, so whole pictures and big tiles ran out of memory (and, earlier, the cuDNN engine problem gave garbage: fixed by `_cudnn_probe`). Now `gpu._cuda_total()` is the memory this process may plan with: min(total, free + what it already holds). An out-of-memory in the development retries the picture in tiles of half the planned side, then on the CPU (`develop`); in the sharpening it falls to the CPU; only a forced `backend='gpu'` still raises. Checked on the real card with `scripts/gpu_parity.py` (DSC00014.ARW, auto route): half size 56 s CPU -> 4.4 s GPU, full size (24 MP, tiled at 2048) 201 s -> 15.4 s; smooth component of the difference (8 px blur) max 0.0069 (1.8 levels), mean 0.0003; the unblurred difference is grain only (mean 0.007). So the GPU route is usable on this machine; `STRATASTILLS_GPU=0` is no longer needed here.

## Lens gaps: the camera's distortion table explained, the Samyang 50, the image-circle crop (scripts/fit_distortion.py)
`scripts/fit_distortion.py` registers our uncorrected development to the camera JPEG (optical flow on edge maps) and reports the radial displacement at the corner in % of the half diagonal; run with `--mode geometry` / `--patch` it shows what is left after a correction. Precision about 0.01-0.05 %. (1) **Samyang AF 35 and AF 50 JPEGs are not distortion-corrected** (corner shift 0.00 % over 4 and 5 pictures; the camera's distortion setting is 0 for these lenses), so `lens: camera` rightly does nothing for them and lensfun's correction of the 35 moves the picture 1.1 % at the corner away from the JPEG, as intended (a true lens correction). ## Learned denoise on big pictures and DJI files (2026-10-25)
- Found: any picture over the card's pixel budget (11 MP on the GTX 1050, so every 48 MP DJI file and every full-size A7C one) took the tiled route, which never ran the learned model. The tiled route now collects the noise map per tile and the median of the tiles' blind noise, then runs `denoise_ai.adaptive` on the result like the whole-picture route (full-size A7C ISO 3200: 199 s, 24 MP, model on).
- DJI files: the blind noise before the clean-up is 2.1-2.6 on ten of eleven, so the old trigger (>= 1.5) would have run the model on all of them; after the classical clean-up they measure 0.0-0.7 (the camera JPEG 1.7, A7C after the clean-up 0.8-1.2 from ISO 500 up). With the model on one DJI file went 0.61 -> 0.34 noise and lost 7 % of the fine detail, at three times the time. So `denoise: auto` now also asks for the noise left after the clean-up (`AUTO_AI_LEFT` 0.8), and goes straight to the model above 4.0 before it (`AUTO_AI_FLAT`; the post estimate reads 0.00 when the flat areas are cored to nothing). A7C behaviour is unchanged by construction.

## The blotchy texture after sharpening (2026-10-25)
Seen in the report on NEX-5N pictures (and as scalloped curved edges on some DJI crops): smooth, low-contrast textures (a stone ground, water) come out as cells or cobbles after the sharpen stage; the stages before it are smooth.
- Cause: not the filter shape. The Wiener gain is `((1+n) m / (m^2+n))^amount`, with amount 3, capped at 4: on a picture whose measured MTF is low it sits at the cap over a wide band (NEX-5N 2012-03-11_DSC00656: MTF 0.26 at 0.1 and 0.11 at 0.2 cycles/px of native, gain 4.0 from 0.08 of output up). The measurement of a mostly soft scene describes the scene, not the lens, and the boost lifts the mottling of low-contrast texture into blobs; the clamp to the local 5x5 range (OVERSHOOT 0) then pins 28 % of the pixels to the extremes (5 % on a sharp A7C picture), which makes the cells flat. Raising OVERSHOOT to 0.1 removes the clamping and the blobs stay (rounder); a lower amount or max gain only lessens them.
- What works: raise the activity mask (the gradient below which nothing is sharpened, `SHARPEN_MASK` (2, 6) levels/px): (6, 18) or (8, 24) leave the cobbles out and keep the sharpened edges. On the whole A7C and DJI sets that mask costs sharpness and detail (A7C sharp 19.7 -> 16.9, detail 2.67 -> 2.22, ring 4.7 -> 4.1; DJI sharp 52.0 -> 50.4), and the detail metric counts the blobs as detail, so it cannot judge this by itself.
- Fix: `detail.sharpen_mask(mtf_freq, mtf)`: the mask is multiplied by MASK_REF_MTF (0.45) / the MTF at 0.2 cycles/px native, between 1 and 3.5; a picture that measures sharp enough keeps (2, 6). A7C and DJI medians are unchanged to two decimals (their pictures measure 0.5 or more there, except a few DJI ones at 0.2-0.4 that get a mild raise), the NEX-5N ground and water crops come out clean. Sharpen version 6.
- Also seen, not looked at: the NEX-5N QR-code picture (2012-03-31_DSC00763) comes out much flatter and darker than the camera JPEG (the tone is not matched for this camera: no profile, 'unknown device'), and the camera JPEG there is only the small preview in the raw.

## Adding a device: `scripts/add_device.py`, and the NEX-5N profile (2026-10-26)
A new camera is rare, so the aim is the best result for it, not full automation: one repeatable script (`scripts/add_device.py <folder> <candidates.json> --make --model [--noise noiseprofiles.json] [--dcp x.dcp]`) runs the steps that already existed as separate tools: `bundle` (colour matrices from a DCP or, when none exists, LibRaw's own; darktable's noise table; the `inputs.PROFILES` line), `tone` (fit_tone_curve), `look` (fit_look_table, every 5th picture held out), `noise` (fit_noise_scale). Each step can be rerun alone (`--steps`).
- **NEX-5N:** RawTherapee ships no DCP for it, darktable has noise fits. The archive holds 33k NEX-5N raws (kit PZ 16-50 18k, 35 f/1.8 6k, 50 f/1.8 2.7k, 16 f/2.8 2.5k, 55-210 1.7k, manual 1.2k) and every one checked has its JPEG beside it. The set is now 124 (the 33 plus 91 picked by ISO band x lens x shoot and screened for people with YOLO), up to ISO 5000: the camera hardly went above it (78 raws), so nothing public is left above.
- **What the fit gave** (held out, every 5th): hue error 1.2 -> 0.0 degrees, brightest tones dE 4.1 -> 2.9, median dE 3.4 -> 3.3. A least-squares 3x3 on top of the look table gained about 0.2 dE: the rest is the camera's tone mapping, not colour, so no matrix fit. The QR-code picture that looked flat now tracks the camera JPEG.
- **Noise:** our flat-area noise against the camera JPEG's falls with the scale then flattens near 1.3 at base ISO (a floor the profile does not set), so the fit extrapolated to 128. `fit_noise_scale.py` now stays inside the scales tried and leaves out bands with fewer than 3 pictures; `--per-band N` limits the pictures per band (a full 124-picture fit takes hours: the learned denoise is about a minute per picture on the GTX 1050). Stored: ISO 103/567 -> 32, 1581 -> 15.3, 3200 -> 28.4.
- **A7C noise baseline, checked:** the 1.13 against 0.73 was not a regression. On 8 pictures the code of PR #8 and today's give the same flat-area noise (1.15 / 1.09 unsharpened; 1.24 / 1.15 sharpened); the 0.73 was another render run (`q95-a7c-new`).
